# IRONHOLD — *The Siege Week*
*(working title — the name is easy to change later)*

> **Seven days. One empty throne. Trade or be trampled.**

---

## 1. Logline

Every seven days the crown of a shattered empire is up for the taking. You are a
lord on a scrap of land that produces **one** thing the world needs. You cannot win
alone, you cannot even *build* alone — so you scheme: who to befriend, who to feed,
who to raid, and who to trade with. At the end of the week, whoever holds the most
**Conquest Points (CP)** is crowned. Then the crown shatters, and it begins again.

This is a **slow, thinking person's war game** — played in a browser, in short
check-ins across the day. Nothing needs to be animated; everything is maps, numbers,
timers, and decisions. The drama is in the diplomacy, not the fireworks.

---

## 2. The world: the Sundered Crown

The old empire had one High King and one Iron Crown. When the last king died with no
heir, the Crown did something no one expected: it **broke** — not into pieces of metal,
but into a curse. It cannot rest on any head for more than seven days before it
shatters and scatters its authority back across the land.

So the realm lives in an endless cycle it calls **the Siege Week**. Every seven days
the shards of the Crown reassemble in the sky over the old capital, and every lord in
the land races to gather enough **Conquest Points** — proof of might, wealth, and
influence — to claim it. Whoever holds the most at the final bell is crowned…
for a week. Then the Crown breaks again, the map is redrawn, and the war begins anew.

*(In game terms: this is your **season**. A season is one week. Season reset is not a
chore — it is the entire premise. It also keeps the game — and the database — small,
because last week's war is written into legend, not kept on disk.)*

---

## 3. You, the Lord of a single Hold

You rule one **Fortress** ("your Hold") on one patch of the broken land. Here is the
cruel joke of the Sundered Crown: **each patch of land produces exactly one raw
material, and only one.** Your neighbor sits on **Ironwood**; you sit on **Skysteel**;
the lord across the river sits on **Saltpeter**. There is no market where you can just
*buy* what you lack, because everyone lacks something.

- You gather your one material by working your land (**Harvest**).
- You raise an **army** and march it out to raid or to defend (**March / Assault /
  Bulwark**).
- You **level up** as you play, growing stronger, unlocking skills, and fielding more
  troops.
- And above all, you **trade** — because nothing worth building can be built from one
  material alone.

---

## 4. Why you cannot win alone (the beating heart)

Every real prize in Ironhold — a stronger fortress, better war-gear, the recipes that
win battles — costs a **mix** of materials. And the mix always contains at least one
material your land does not produce. That is deliberate.

It means you are constantly asking the only questions that matter in this game:

- **Who do I make a friend of?** (so their material flows to me)
- **Who do I trade with?** (my surplus for their scarcity)
- **Who do I attack?** (to *take* what I cannot buy — but raiding makes enemies)
- **Who do I leave alone?** (because I need them next week too)

A lord who hoards and attacks everyone will find no one will trade — and starve of the
materials they can't make. A lord who only trades and never fights will be farmed by
raiders. The winner is the **strategist and diplomat**, not the biggest army.

---

## 5. Your commanders: two legends who ride for you

Commanders are rare, storied figures. You do not simply hire them — you win their
loyalty with **Gems** (a mark of favor and renown) or earn them through **Quests**.
When the Crown first shatters and you begin a new season, **both commanders ride with
you free for the first 12 hours each**, as a trial of allegiance. After that, you must
keep them the hard way.

### Durgan Stonesworn, the Steward *(the dwarf)*
An ancient dwarven war-steward who has served a hundred fallen lords. Durgan's gift is
that he is never caught wearing the wrong armor. Give him your Hold and he **reads the
moment and swaps your gear for you, automatically**:

- Your troops are marching out to **attack** → he dons **Assault** gear.
- Someone is **attacking you** → he throws on **Bulwark** (defence)… then switches
  *back* to Assault the instant he remembers you have a raid of your own still in the
  field.
- Your army is **on the road** → he switches to **March** gear so they arrive faster.
- You've **stepped away / gone idle** → he quietly puts on **Harvest** gear so your
  land keeps earning while you sleep.

Without Durgan you can still do all of this by hand — but you'll be a step slow, and in
a seven-day war a step slow is a lost throne.

### Wren Nightglass, the Scout
A ranger who sees what others hide. Send Wren against a rival and she brings back a
**report**: how big their army really is, what gear they're wearing, how well their
Hold is defended, and what materials they're sitting on. In a game built on *whom to
trust*, Wren is how you stop guessing. Knowledge is the sharpest blade here.

---

## 6. Gems, chests, and the climb

- **Gems** are favor made solid. You earn a few by **watching a raven's tribute** (an
  ad), by seizing the **wild mines** that erupt across the map, and as **quest** and
  daily rewards. You spend them on the things that are meant to be rare — recruiting a
  commander, forcing open an extra chest, sending an extra scout.
- **Chests** are how the land rewards you with **gear**. They come in grades — a humble
  **Wood** chest, then **Iron**, **Gold**, and the coveted **Royal** chest — richer
  chests holding rarer, stronger gear. You earn them from quests, from leveling up, and
  from the map.
- **Leveling** is your personal climb: fight, gather, trade, and finish quests to earn
  XP; each new level grants **skill points** (spent on a small tree of edges — sharper
  attacks, tougher walls, faster harvests) and **fresh troops**, and now and then a
  chest falls into your lap.

---

## 7. The shape of a week

- **Days 1–2 — Settle.** Your commanders are free; learn your land, harvest, open your
  first chests, feel out your neighbors, sign your first trades.
- **Days 3–5 — Scheme.** Alliances form and break. Trade routes matter. Scouts fly.
  The first real raids land. This is where the season is won or lost — quietly.
- **Days 6–7 — Storm the throne.** Conquest Points are everything now. Betrayals are
  forgiven or repaid. The leaderboard is a live thing everyone is watching.
- **The final bell.** The lord with the most CP is **crowned** — a title, a place in
  the season's legend, and a small blessing to carry into next week. Then the Crown
  shatters, the map is wiped clean, and a new Siege Week dawns.

---

## 8. How it looks and feels (built for the browser)

Ironhold is deliberately a game of **maps, panels, numbers, and timers** — not of
animated battles. That is a feature, not a compromise:

- A **map** of your corner of the realm: your Hold, your neighbors, wild mines, and
  the roads between them.
- **Panels** for your gear loadouts, inventory, army, level, skills, and commanders.
- **Timers** everywhere — gathers finishing, armies arriving, trades pending, chests
  ready. You check in, make decisions, and let time do the rest, like a chess game
  played by mail.
- **Reports** — the story of what happened while you were away: raids resolved, scouts
  returned, trades accepted.

You can play it in five minutes on a phone between meetings, and it never needs a
single frame of animation to be tense. The tension is all in the *decisions*.

---

## 9. One line to remember

> *In Ironhold, your army wins battles — but your **friends** win the week.*
